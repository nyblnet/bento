bento/id privacy notice

Effective __EFFECTIVE_DATE__ · Controller: __CONTROLLER__ · Contact: __CONTACT_EMAIL__

bento/id lets the people you work with check that a document or an edit really came from you. Your account is your email address and a list of passkeys on your own devices. There is no password, and no secret of yours is kept on our side.

Three services take part, and this notice covers all three:

  • id.bento.page, the directory. It keeps your account's list of passkeys and sends the email described below.
  • signin.id.bento.page, the sign-in page. It is where you create and use your passkeys.
  • home.bento.page, bento/home in a browser, which can sign in with bento/id.

Bento documents are files you keep. bento/id never stores them or anything inside them.

Items marked Planned describe parts that are not running yet. They are here so you know what is coming, and this notice will be updated before any of them starts.

Your account: a list of passkeys

A passkey is made by your device, or by your password manager. Its private part never leaves it. For each passkey on your account, the directory keeps:

  • its public key, and the identifier your device gave it;
  • a counter your device increases each time the passkey is used, which helps us notice a copied passkey;
  • when it was added, and whether that was at setup, from a browser or from an app.

The directory also keeps every version of the list: your address, the public key of each passkey on it, and when the version was made. When a change takes effect, the directory signs that version with its own key; we call this its countersignature. When one of your passkeys asked for the change, the passkey's signed request is kept with that version. Each version points to the one before it. That chain is how anyone can check that the list they see now is the one that grew from your setup.

This list is public. Anyone who knows your address can ask the directory whether it has an account, and read the current list: the passkeys' public keys, when the list last changed, and whether a change is waiting. Nobody can browse or search the directory. Without your exact address there is nothing to find.

How long: the current list, and every earlier version, for as long as the account exists. This includes earlier versions that name passkeys you have since removed. When a passkey is removed, its identifier, counter and added date are deleted at once.

Setting up, by emailed link

To set up an account you ask for a setup link, and the directory emails it to your address. You open it, and make a passkey on the sign-in page. You never type or paste a code: no bento/id email ever asks you to.

  • The secret in the link sits in the part of the address that browsers do not send to any server. We keep only a one-way hash of it.
  • A link works for 20 minutes, or for 24 hours when it was sent beyond the usual limit (below). It stops working once it is used. The hashes go when the account is set up.
  • Anyone can ask for a setup link for any address, but it always goes to that address, and only up to one a minute, three a day and ten in 31 days. To apply these limits we keep the times the last setup links were sent to an address, and the hashes of links that expired unused, even when no account was ever made: __SETUP_TIMES_RETENTION__

The email we send you

Every change to your account is mailed to its address. That includes the setup, a change that is waiting, a change that took effect, a cancelled change, a suspended passkey, and a reset. Each of these emails lists every passkey on the account, by a short fingerprint, with when and how it was added. That way an addition you did not make can't hide among the others.

The emails are fixed wording. They contain no tracking images and no tracked links. Their only links are the ones described here, and every link opens a page that does nothing until you press a button on it.

A change waits 72 hours, and you can stop it

Adding or removing a passkey takes effect 72 hours after it is asked for, by the directory's clock. The email saying it is waiting carries a link with two choices. Cancel this change is always available. The second, Cancel, and remove the passkey that asked for it, is offered only when another passkey would remain. A passkey removing itself takes effect at once, as long as another passkey remains.

  • The secret in that link is like the setup link's. We keep only its hash, and keep it only while the change is waiting. It is deleted when the change takes effect or is stopped.
  • If you cancel, the passkey that asked cannot ask for another change for 72 hours. We keep that end time with the passkey.
  • If you remove the passkey that asked, it is suspended at once. It is removed 72 hours later. The email that tells you so carries a link that puts it back. That link's hash is kept only until the removal takes effect.

If you lose every passkey: the reset

You can ask for a reset link to be emailed to your address. On the sign-in page you make a new passkey. The reset then waits 72 hours, and its email carries a link that stops it. It replaces any change that was waiting. When it takes effect, the new passkey is the only one on the account, and the earlier versions of the list stay in the chain (above). In an account's first 14 days only that emailed link can stop a reset. After that, one of the account's passkeys will also be able to stop it. Planned Stopping a reset with a passkey is not running yet.

A reset link works for 24 hours, and one is sent at most while no other is live. We keep only its hash. It is deleted when a reset starts. An expired one can stay until the next reset link is asked for.

The record of changes

The directory keeps a record of every setup, every passkey added, and every change asked for, cancelled, suspended, put back, replaced by a reset or taken effect. Each entry holds:

  • what happened, and when;
  • which passkey it was about, and which passkey asked for it (their public keys);
  • the web address the request came from, and the address of the page where the passkey was used;
  • which page asked the sign-in page to act, when it was bento/home. That page is the only one allowed to ask.

It holds no IP address and no document. It exists so that, if one of our own services were ever misused, we could find the changes it made and offer you a way to undo them. It is not public. How long: for the life of the account.

Planned Signing in bento/home in a browser

Planned bento/home in a browser makes two keys of its own, which cannot be copied out of that browser, and keeps them in that browser's storage for home.bento.page, with your address and its certificate. To sign it in, you approve with one of your passkeys on the sign-in page. The directory then records the sign-in (in the record above) and issues a certificate that is valid for 24 hours. bento/home renews it while you use it, but never beyond 7 days after you approved. After that you approve again, and fresh keys are made. Signing out deletes the keys from that browser. A later option will sign out every browser at once.

What the sign-in page keeps in your browser

The sign-in page sets no cookies. It keeps one item in your browser's storage for signin.id.bento.page: your account's address, under the name bento-id-account, so that it asks the directory about the right account. It stays in that browser until you clear the site's data. Planned The pages that save it are not running yet.

Planned A lapse changes only the label. Others see “unconfirmed since” and a date, instead of the full ✓. Your account, its passkeys, its browser sign-ins and your documents keep working, and nothing is cancelled or suspended. Confirming later restores the label at once. For this we keep the date of your last confirmation, when the next link is due, the date of any lapse, and for an organisation's request, when it was made, which organisation made it and a one-way hash identifying the administrator. Each email sent and each answer is added to the record of changes. All of this is kept for the life of the account.

Network information

To stop abuse, the directory limits how often one network can ask it for anything. It keeps, per hour, a counter under a one-way fingerprint of the IP address. The fingerprint is made with a secret that changes every day, so one day's fingerprints cannot be matched to the next day's. The counters and that day's secret are deleted a minute after the day ends (UTC), or at the latest a day and a minute after it ends. IP addresses themselves are not stored.

Each request to change an account carries a one-time challenge from the directory. Challenges are not stored. A used one is remembered for 10 minutes, so it cannot be used again.

What bento/id never stores

  • Your documents, or anything inside them.
  • Any private key: neither your passkeys' nor bento/home's.
  • A password. There isn't one.
  • Who you share with, or a list of your contacts.
  • Who looked up which address.
  • Any code for you to type.
